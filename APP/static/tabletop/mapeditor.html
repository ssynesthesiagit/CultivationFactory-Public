Materials

Floors change only the look. Named terrain also works in fights: difficult ground, obscurement, and what moving through it does.

Trees

A trunk blocks movement and sight like a wall; the canopy is only the look. Bamboo costs twice as much movement and makes sight-based Perception checks harder (Disadvantage). Click a tree again to remove it.

Scenery heights

Height is measured in feet above the base. Fly above the top to clear an obstacle. Zero means no vertical obstruction. Editing height keeps the object's open state and blocking rules. Raised scenery also shows its top above map ground.

    Click a placed object to edit it.

    Draw a straight wall, then place a door, gate or window on it to make a one-square opening: it snaps to the wall line nearest your pointer (an edge or the middle of the square), and the map shows where it will land. Select any object and press Q/E to rotate it. Erase uses X.

    Objects

    Placed objects can be attacked and destroyed in a fight. Closed doors and gates block movement and sight; opening one clears its square. A window placed in a wall also blocks the opening unless you check Open. Barricades, wall sections, pillars, statues, ice blocks and carts fill their square until destroyed. Numbers come from the 2024 object tables (armor class by material, hit points by size; immune to poison and psychic). Click an object to select it, then rotate or remove it above.

    Small things can share a square: a 5-foot square holds up to 4 tiny objects (a cushion, a sack), 2 small ones (a crate, a barrel), 1 small and 2 tiny, or 1 medium or large object on its own. Click a free spot in the square, Shift+click an object, or use "Add picked object beside it"; they are arranged to look natural, and you can drag one to nudge it within its square. Each object keeps its own rules: the square blocks movement and sight if any object in it does, and attacking one object leaves the others standing. Decor pieces take no space.

    Decor

    Scenery only changes the look, except pieces marked (cover) or (difficult): those are ordinary cover (+2 AC) or difficult ground (each foot of movement costs one extra foot), the same as the Terrain tool. Click a placed piece with this tool to remove it.

    Terrain

    Background

    Roofs cover enclosed indoor floors. In character view they fade when your creature enters; walls and doors still decide what you can see.

    Climbing spends extra movement. Mark a difficult wall only when its surface calls for an Athletics check.

    Roofs cover enclosed indoor floors. Your creature can stand on top at the roof's height. The roof fades for you only when your creature is below it inside the building.

    Trapdoors and skylights

    Each opening fills one square. Open it to pass through the roof. Closed trapdoors are solid; closed skylights let you see through glass but block passage and effects. Both closed types support a creature.

    This map

    Walls are drawn as shapes. The rules turn them into squares (dark tint): a square is a wall when the shape covers its centre (filled shapes) or the line passes through it. Line of sight is checked square by square, the same way for every fighter and for the fog of war.
